## How to use the online metronome

1. **Set the BPM.** Adjust the tempo (beats per minute) from 1 to 240 with the slider or the buttons. You can also tap the tempo by hand with the "Tap tempo" button.
2. **Choose the number of beats.** Most music has 4, 3 or 2 beats per bar (4/4, 3/4, 2/4). Choose 1 if you are not sure.
3. **Press "Start".** The metronome starts immediately. To stop after a while, turn on the timer. The Space key also starts and stops.

## Features

- **BPM from 1 to 240.** From Larghissimo to Prestissimo. The tempo marking appears below the number, so you can relate the BPM to the marking on the score.
- **Beats per bar with accent.** From 1 to 12 beats, with the first one accented. Turn the accent off for an even pulse, useful for internal-time exercises.
- **Subdivisions.** Eighth notes, triplets, sixteenth notes and patterns with rests, shown as musical figures. Subdivision clicks are softer than the main beat.
- **Tap tempo.** Tap in rhythm and the metronome works out the tempo from the average of your last taps. Use it as a BPM counter for a song or to set the tempo for a band before counting in.
- **Practice timer.** Sets how many minutes the metronome plays before stopping by itself. Good for timed practice sessions and for practicing without looking at the screen.
- **Visual beat indicator.** The beats light up on screen in sync with the sound. Helps players of loud instruments, such as drums, and anyone practicing at low volume.
- **Speed trainer.** Raises (or lowers) the BPM automatically every N bars until the target tempo. Set it up under "More options".
- **Muted bars.** Plays some bars and mutes others while keeping count, to train your internal time.
- **Four click sounds.** Beep, wood, clave and mechanical tick. The visual flash lights the whole screen on the beat, useful in full screen.
- **Saved settings and presets.** Your last settings come back on the next visit. Save named presets for each song and share the configuration by link.
- **Light and dark theme.** The choice is saved in your browser.
- **Keyboard shortcuts.** Space starts and stops; the arrows adjust the BPM; T taps the tempo.

## What is a metronome for?

A metronome keeps a steady pulse, measured in beats per minute (BPM). Its job is not to play for you, but to show you honestly when you rush or drag. Over time, your ear starts to notice those deviations without the click: that is what we call internal time.

In practice, a metronome is used for three things:

1. **Practicing difficult passages.** Starting slowly and raising the tempo little by little, the body learns the right movement before gaining speed. That is the method described in [How to practice with a metronome](/en/how-to-practice-with-a-metronome/).
2. **Developing rhythmic precision.** Exercises such as playing only on beats 2 and 4, shifting the click to the offbeat or reducing it to one click per bar train internal time directly.
3. **Setting the right tempo.** Before rehearsing or recording, the metronome gives you the reference BPM of the song. The [tempo markings guide](/en/tempo-markings/) explains what each speed means and in which styles it appears.

## Accuracy

Many browser metronomes use ordinary JavaScript timers, which drift when the page loses focus. This metronome schedules every click on the **Web Audio API** clock, the same one used by recording software, with sub-millisecond precision. If the click seems irregular, the cause is almost always external: Bluetooth headphones, which add variable latency, another tab using the processor, or battery saving on a phone.
